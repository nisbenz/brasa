    How the numbers are made

    MODIS has watched fires since 2000. VIIRS, its successor, sees smaller fires with sharper pixels, so it reports about four times as many hotspots. Brasa rewrites the MODIS years in VIIRS terms so that 2003 and 2023 can sit in the same calendar.

    1. Count fires on a 25 km grid

      Every detection lands in a 0.25° cell and a month. Only presumed vegetation fires are kept: volcanoes, gas flares and other fixed heat sources are removed, as are low-confidence detections.

    2. Learn how VIIRS and MODIS differ, cell by cell

      From 2012 to 2024 both sensors flew. For each cell, Brasa learns how many VIIRS hotspots correspond to one MODIS hotspot. Cells with few fires borrow from their neighbourhood (1°, then 5°, then the globe), and the ratio shifts with the season.

    3. Add the fires MODIS could not see

      When MODIS saw nothing in a cell, VIIRS often still caught small fires. Brasa adds the number VIIRS typically finds in that situation. Leaving this out undercounts by several percent.

    4. Rewrite 2000–2011, keep VIIRS from 2012

      Before Aqua launched (to June 2002) only Terra flew, so a separate Terra-only model is used. Months with satellite outages are scaled by days observed, and the two Suomi NPP outages (August 2022, July 2024) use NOAA-20.

    Does it work?

    Trained on 2012–2018 only, then asked to predict 2019–2024 from MODIS and compared with what VIIRS actually saw. The last row shows two VIIRS satellites compared with each other: no conversion can do better than that.

    Error is the median of |ln((predicted+1)/(observed+1))|; 0.21 means a typical miss of about 23%.
